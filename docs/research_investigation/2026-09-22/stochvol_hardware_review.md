# Stochastic-volatility pricing and hardware: adversarial investigation

Search date: 2026-09-22. This is a bounded primary-source investigation, prioritizing 2024-09 onward and checking recent 2026 work. It is not exhaustive. No manuscript claims were changed. Files in this directory contain new development diagnostics only.

**Verdict: No defensible significant quantum advantage established yet.** Replacing Gaussian preparation with weak Euler is a real quantum implementation improvement, already published, but its existing resource constants fail an inexpensive classical rejection check. The remaining direct-pricing question should concern a substantially cheaper **coherent multilevel correction**, with proven coupling and a variance-sensitive estimator, rather than another single-level weak-Euler circuit. A positive contribution would require a conditional physical crossover against optimized classical integration, not just a better exponent against plain MC.

## What the published stochastic-volatility circuit actually costs

The anchor is Wang–Kan Tables 3–7, Eqs. 51–52,64,92–94,125–126. Asian instance 1 has 256 steps, cap/normalizer Z=200, AE precision 0.001 and confidence 90%; the weak circuit uses 2.4e11 T gates, T-depth 1.2e11 and 22,000 logical qubits. Its tabulated normalized error 0.0013 becomes approximately $0.2523 after discounting, before continuous-model bias and payoff-tail repair. Appendix A details arithmetic; B includes synthesis/catalyst costs; C explains why Gaussian loading was expensive. These are resource estimates, not executed fault-tolerant pricing. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15871v3).

My derived lower-bound sensitivity, ignoring Clifford, factory, routing, measurement and decoding overhead, is:

| Effective logical T-layer time, hypothetical | Published-depth runtime lower bound | Classical runtime necessary for a 10? quantum win |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1 microsecond | 12,000s =3.33h | 120,000s =33.3h |
| 1 microsecond | 120,000s =33.3h | 1,200,000s =13.9days |
| 10 microseconds | 1,200,000s =13.9days | 12,000,000s =139days |

These latencies are sensitivity inputs, not vendor demonstrations. The price is one classical scalar; simultaneous execution of independent AE experiments changes hardware demand and latency, not total gate work. The paper's depth is its serial resource convention, not a lower bound over every possible future architecture. A redesigned parallel schedule must be explicit and costed.

For a new algorithm, write

`T_Q = setup_Q + sum_l repetitions_l * max(D_l*tau_T, N_T,l/(F_l*r_factory)) + measurement + decoding`,

with dependency-correct scheduling, including state preparation and uncomputation in each `D_l,N_T,l`. Compare with the measured optimized classical runtime `T_C(epsilon,delta,workload)`, including controls/reference setup under the same amortization contract. Require `T_Q <= T_C/10` over held-out parameter neighborhoods. Tenfold is a prospective practical threshold reflecting hardware/model uncertainty, distinct from statistical confidence; a lower threshold would need a different business justification.

For a variance-sensitive level estimator, a useful algebraic screening model is `q_l ~ a_l sigma_l/epsilon_l` (with actual estimator logarithms/range terms restored before claims). Let `c_l` be seconds per coherent level query and `w_l=a_l*sigma_l*c_l`. Minimizing `sum_l w_l/epsilon_l` under `sum_l epsilon_l <= epsilon_stat` gives `epsilon_l proportional sqrt(w_l)` and quantum time approximately `(sum_l sqrt(w_l))^2/epsilon_stat`. Classical iid MLMC instead gives approximately `(sum_l sigma_l*sqrt(cC_l))^2/epsilon_stat^2`, with appropriate confidence factors. These are a cost-allocation model, not a theorem about the current implementation or the strongest RQMC competitor. Both fixed-point and tail errors may force larger query costs at fine levels.

## Executed rejection diagnostics

The [frozen empirical protocol](heston_pilot_protocol.md), [script](heston_discrete_pilot.py) and [results](heston_discrete_pilot_results.json) use the published weak-Euler update and fixing convention, but make the target **explicitly capped** at 200. They do not reproduce quantum fixed-point arithmetic and do not certify the continuous model. All random generation, path evolution and an independent control-fit cost were timed. CPU thread environment limits were 1.

| Method, 16 independent replicates of 4096 antithetic pairs each | Capped discrete price | Empirical 95% Student-t half-width | Wall time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Vectorized MC |13.922532|0.058723|2.336s|
| Chronological scrambled Sobol, thresholded to signs |13.917784|0.012859|4.715s|
| Sobol plus independent fitted exact-mean log-return control |13.917853|0.012871|4.824s including training|

The control did not improve Sobol in this diagnostic. There is no independent reference price. The confidence intervals above are empirical across 16 randomizations and must not be equated to the quantum AE guarantee. These elementary baselines are far from the strongest continuous-Heston comparators.

To avoid relying on that mismatch, a **separately frozen** [Hoeffding protocol](heston_hoeffding_protocol.md), [script](heston_hoeffding_pilot.py) and [result](heston_hoeffding_pilot_results.json) used 1,111,698 iid antithetic pairs. With pair payoff range `R=200 exp(-.03)`, the nonasymptotic statistical radius is `R sqrt(log(2/.05)/(2M)) <= $.25`. It completed in 45.249s, yielding 13.924625. This guarantee assumes ideal iid randomness and exact payoff evaluation; the implementation uses PCG64 and floating point and has not certified rounding error. It also excludes continuous-model and uncapped-tail error. The published circuit's most optimistic table sensitivity remains about 265 times slower than this conservative classical statistical certificate; a 10? projected win would require at least roughly 2652-fold reduction of its serial depth at that 100ns assumption before other costs.

Four sampled paths exceeded 200, with maximum payoff 213.10. This directly demonstrates why a sample maximum cannot be treated as a hard support bound. A small exceedance probability alone does not bound expected omitted payoff. For uncapped calls, use a proved moment/tail inequality or separately certified tail contribution. For puts, the contractual payoff bound can be exact.

These results reject reuse of the old circuit as the current advantage claim. They do not prove that every coherent Heston method loses, nor that a continuous-price computation with a strict numerical certificate has been completed.

## The promising reopening requires a different mechanism

The root investigation identifies a possible opening in coherent antithetic multilevel corrections: for one common Gaussian increment register, calculate fine path, fine path with paired increments swapped, and coarse path; return `Delta_l = (P_fine+P_swapped)/2-P_coarse` coherently. This is an algorithm hypothesis. It removes a potentially costly Lévy-area requirement only if the corresponding classical antithetic argument survives the actual model and reversible approximation.

The existing signed-residual infrastructure is relevant, but do not encode `Delta_l` by adding a large positive offset and then cite small classical variance as a quantum benefit. Implement an estimator whose stated complexity depends on the signed second moment/variance, including any scale estimation and truncation. Charge two fine evolutions, one coarse evolution, controls, reversible cleanup, reflections, and each AE repetition.

Classical antithetic MLMC gets the same correction. Classical randomized multilevel QMC additionally gets common factors, smoothing and geometric controls. If its observed error approaches inverse work, a quantum `epsilon^-1.25` regime may lose even asymptotically on the tested workload. An exponent against plain MC is therefore insufficient.

The model is not covered merely by invoking Feller: derivatives of square-root variance are singular near zero. Any cutoff changes the model and needs a bias allowance. A call payoff kink, monitoring convention and path average need their own weak-error and correction-variance proof or carefully labeled empirical estimates. Heston geometric-control transforms refer to the same continuous model, but using their expectation with discretized paths still requires discrepancy accounting.

**Do not merge this idea with naïve Rademacher weak Euler.** Let two fine increments be independent ±sqrt(h); their sum is in {-2sqrt(h),0,2sqrt(h)}, while a coarse weak-Euler increment is ±sqrt(2h). Even the best sign/tie coupling has mean-square discrepancy `(4-2sqrt(2))*h` per coarse interval. Over T/(2h) independent intervals the terminal discrepancy remains `(2-sqrt(2))*T`, rather than tending to zero. This elementary calculation invalidates an automatic transfer of the usual strong-coupling variance rate. Weak MLMC literature has remedies using level-dependent laws; those remedies and their coherent preparation cost must be implemented and are prior art.

My recommended next calculation is a costed antithetic-correction variance study with shared Gaussian increments, comparing ordinary MLMC and multilevel RQMC, while a parallel symbolic circuit estimate sets the maximum allowable logical query cost. If that allowable cost is below a defensible floor for two path evolutions, stop before circuit engineering. The published weak circuit alone is no longer a reason to open a six-week Heston implementation project.

For scale, a union-bound budget of 1% for 2.4e11 injected T states requires each injected-state failure contribution no larger than about 4.2e-14, before data-memory, Clifford and decoder failures. Use an actual architecture resource estimator and an instruction-level failure budget, not these illustrations, in a level 2 paper.

Prioritized sections to read before implementing anything:

1. Wang–Kan §§3.2,4.1,4.3 footnote 8,4.6–4.7 and Tables 5–7; Appendices A–C. They expose numerical-target and resource accounting gaps.

4. arxiv2609.07169 §§3.1/3.3,5.1, Eq. 54 and Table 6. Charge independent control fitting and transform/reference error. Its 99.936% variance reduction is a single reported Heston test, not a universal constant.
5. Belomestny–Nagapetyan §3 and Debrabant et al.'s weak-antithetic construction before trying cheap discrete increments across levels.
6. IBM Tour de gross §4 and Appendix A.10; IonQ Walking Cat §§VIII,XV,XVII,XIX,XXI. These connect logical resources to space/time and reveal why qubit counts alone are inadequate.
