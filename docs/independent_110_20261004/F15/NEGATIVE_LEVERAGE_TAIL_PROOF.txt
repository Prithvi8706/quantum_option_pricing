Method1, affine-MGF supersolution. For p>1,lambda>=0 define
M_t=exp(-(p*r+kappa*theta*lambda)t) S_t^p exp(lambda*v_t).
Ito gives its drift coefficient, multiplyingv_tM_t,
q(p,lambda)=xi^2 lambda^2/2+(rho*xi*p-kappa)lambda+p(p-1)/2.
Ifq<=0, stop at the usual bounded S/v/stochastic-integral localization times.
The stopped positive process is a supermartingale. Fatou after localization
yields E M_t<=S0^p exp(lambda*v0); no unproved true-martingale/Novikov equality
is needed. Sinceexp(lambda*v)>=1,
E S_t^p <= S0^p exp((p*r+kappa*theta*lambda)t+lambda*v0).
For(p,lambda)=(8,12),q=-98/25; for(2,1),q=-251/200. These inequalities prove
usable finite moments over all twelve dates before Chernoff/Markov is applied.
They assert bounds for these powers and model only, not all Heston moments.

Method2, CIR exponential moments and Holder/Cauchy-Schwarz. Let I_t=int_0^t v_s ds.
Forlambda=1,s=1 the positive process exp(sI_t+v_t-kappa*theta*t) has drift
coefficient xi^2/2-kappa+s=-191/200 timesv_t. The same localization/Fatou argument
gives E exp(I_t)<=exp(v0+kappa*theta*t). Setting s=0 gives E exp(v_t) bounded
by the same quantity; its drift coefficient is-391/200. No observed moment,
sample maximum or assumption of a finite affine transform beyond its domain
is used. This proof supplies an upper-state cutoff, not a lower-endpoint bound.
