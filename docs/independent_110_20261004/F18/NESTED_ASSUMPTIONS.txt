F18: one existing compound-contract theorem map

Contract: d0 E[(E[H|F_tau]-Kc)+], H=d1(A_T-100)+,
d0=exp(-r*tau), d1=exp(-r*(T-tau)), tau=T/2. The fixed C4/C8/H4/H8
development models in compound_feasibility are GBM; H labels do not mean Heston.
The conditional state X consists of spots at tau and accrued fixings. Future
returns R are independent of F_tau in the ideal correlated GBM population.

Primary source: Sun, Wang and Blanchet, Optimal Quantum Speedups for Repeatedly
Nested Expectation Estimation, arXiv2602.08120v1, 8February2026.
https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08120v1
Locators: Section1.2 equation1.1 and Definition1.1; Theorems1.4/1.6; Section3.3.
The version is frozen locally; conference versions are not silently substituted.

The paper assumes fixed depth, unit-cost conditional trajectory access, free
function evaluation, last-argument Lipschitz constants, and a square-integrable
terminal payoff. Theorem1.4 uses L^{p_d}, p_d=2-delta/2^d, with complexity
O(epsilon^{-2(1+delta/2^{d-1})}), delta in(0,1/2). Theorem1.6 uses RMSE and
O(epsilon^{-1} log^{3(D-d)+1}(epsilon^{-1})). Constants and logarithms depend
on the fixed problem; these statements supply no finite circuit or time ratio.

Our mapping, conditional on a vector-block interpretation: y0=X,y1=R,
g1(X,R)=H, gamma1(X)=E[H|X], g0(X,z)=d0(z-Kc)+. Thus D=1,d=0 literally
in equation1.1, with two expectations. Contractual dates12/24 and assets4/8
do not count as nonlinear nesting. A remark in the paper labels an earlier
single-nesting construction D=2; we do not import that convention into this
explicit mapping. The paper writes scalar-coordinate spaces; vector state
blocks need a checked extension or implementable reduction. Neither is claimed
here. A purely measurable real encoding would not establish cheap finite access.

Global outer Lipschitz proof: positive part is1-Lipschitz, so
|g0(X,z)-g0(X,z')|<=d0|z-z'|. The exercise kink requires no derivative under
this condition. This is not a claim that the digital policy is smooth.
Terminal-moment proof: let A=(d*N)^{-1}sum_i,l S_i(t_l). For nonnegative strike,
0<=H<=d1 A and A^2<=(d*N)^{-1}sum_i,l S_i(t_l)^2. Each ideal GBM marginal
has E[S_i(t)^2]=s_i^2 exp(2rt+sigma_i^2t)<infinity by completing the Gaussian
square. No independence of assets or dates is needed for Jensen's inequality.
Conditional moments are finite almost surely by conditional integrability;
no uniform bound over arbitrarily large exercise-date spots follows from this.

Dollar-confidence conversion, derived here: if an actual algorithm has dollar
RMSE<=r about its specified target and deterministic target bias<=b<epsilon,
Markov applied to squared error gives failure<=r^2/(epsilon-b)^2. Hence a
declared statistical allowance alpha requires r^2<=alpha(epsilon-b)^2.
For an L^p norm boundr, replace squares by p powers. Add every separate fault,
normalizer, source-certificate and readout failure to the joint1% allowance.
No independence is needed for this union bound. A theorem's nominal RMSE equal
to epsilon alone permits failure bound1; it does not give99% delivery. The
fixed rational checks illustrate this conversion without revising any active
confidence or dollar allocation. Unknown actual norm constants remain unknown.

Paid costs: classical future-factor reuse charges J*d*n path setup, I*d*n outer
path setup, I*J*d products and each control/payoff calculation. Shared future
samples couple outer outputs; the I*J products are not iid observations. The
existing classical comparator includes geometric controls, policies and nested
MLRQMC; its archived empirical intervals are not promoted to rigorous99% ones.
No classical cubic exponent is required or assumed.

For the quantum candidate, conditional Gaussian preparation, terminal payoff,
source inverse, outer preparation, coherent level processing/median, reflections,
finite arithmetic, synthesis, confidence repetitions and readout must be paid.
The existing quantum_schedule.py is an explicit bounded hierarchy resource
model, not an emitted implementation of the frozen paper's deterministic-level
algorithm. Gaussian clipping/loading, caps and fixed-point changes require
their own dollar-law bridge. No pending A13/A14/A20 or E12 improvement is used.

Decision: this single theorem map reaches its bounded stop rule. Ideal payoff
Lipschitz and integrability are proved; useful error-norm conversions are
provided. Literal block access, finite constants, compatible coherent sources,
joint99% delivery and matched comparator cost remain unresolved. Practical
theorem transfer is closed until those interfaces exist. This neither refutes
the paper nor establishes universal classical or quantum hardness or advantage.
