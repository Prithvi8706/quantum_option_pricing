# E17 workload moment relevance

The current B4x12 and B8x52 contracts use equal-weight GBM baskets at finitely many dates, spot 100, strike 100, volatility 0.3, correlation 0.4, rate 0.03, maturity 1 and barrier 140. Their original payoff is exp(-rT)(A-K)+ times the indicator that every monitored date basket is strictly below H. The archived compound C4/C8/H4/H8 family also uses GBM; H4/H8 are names for higher-volatility, longer-maturity GBM development cases. None is an activated infinite-variance workload. The roadmap instructs one relevance audit and otherwise leaves this branch dormant.

For a GBM marginal S_i(t)=s_i exp((r-sigma_i^2/2)t+sigma_i W_i(t)), completing the square in the Gaussian density gives

    E[S_i(t)^p] = s_i^p exp(p*r*t + p*(p-1)*sigma_i^2*t/2) < infinity.

This holds for every finite positive p and finite t under the stated finite parameters; t=0 and sigma=0 are deterministic limits. It needs no independence between assets or dates. [Sigman's original course notes](https://www.columbia.edu/~ks20/4703-07/4703-07-Notes-BM-GBM-I.pdf), sections 1.2.3 and 1.3, give the GBM exponential representation and increments. The calculation here supplies the general finite-p argument.

For p>=1 and A=sum_l w_l S_l, w_l>=0 and sum_l w_l=1, convexity gives A^p<=sum_l w_l S_l^p. The right side has a finite expectation because there are finitely many fixings. For current nonnegative K, (A-K)+<=A. A bounded digital has every positive moment. Multiplying by a barrier indicator in [0,1] cannot increase any absolute moment. Every deterministic discount factor at finite r,T is finite, including a negative interest rate. For 0<p<1, x^p<=1+x for x>=0 supplies finiteness from the first moment. Thus the ordinary calls, arithmetic averages and monitored barrier payoffs have finite first, second and every finite positive moment, without requiring bounded underlying stock prices.

The actual knockout contract has a stronger law-independent observation: on survival every monitored date basket is below H, so its equal-weight average A is also below H. Therefore its payoff is between zero and exp(-rT)(H-K)+. At H=K or H<K it is identically zero. Equality at any barrier date kills the payoff under the frozen strict convention. This proves boundedness for the payoff even under another nonnegative stock law; it does not prove that an uncapped Heston stock has a finite second moment.

For the compound contract let H_inner=d1(A_T-K)+, C_tau=E[H_inner|F_tau], and J=d0(C_tau-Kc)+. Every selected Kc is nonnegative. Conditional Jensen gives C_tau^p<=E[H_inner^p|F_tau] for p>=1, and hence E[J^p]<=d0^p E[H_inner^p]<infinity. All fixings, including those accrued before tau, stay in A_T. This argument requires no new analytic numerical moment bound, no iid sampling premise, and no future-factor implementation or confidence-sequence change. It proves the true conditional-expectation payoff's integrability, not the correctness of a numerical conditional solver.

The existing signed parity residual is Y=d(h-R). Its derivation assumes positive future levels so A>=G>=0, nonnegative accrued contribution, finite strike/discount, 0<=R<=B, and policy projection that yields 0<=h<=B, with d in {0,1}. Then |Y|<=B and all finite positive moments exist. This is a hypothesis/status audit; it computes no new analytic E10 bound or E12 rule. Remove the policy projection or residual-support premise and this particular boundedness proof no longer applies. Full compound price and surrogate are not thereby bounded. Finite-input native clipping supplies a different support proof under its own law.

Every total map from finitely many finite input words to finite signed w-bit outputs is bounded, regardless of the input probability law. Signed w-bit fixed-point values with f fractional bits have absolute magnitude at most 2^(w-1-f); therefore their first and second moments and every finite positive moment exist. The accepted B4/B8 signed72/f40 outputs have still tighter already-audited all-input support, recorded verbatim in the certificate. This finite-source fact is separate from a continuous-law/roundoff/clipping-bias bridge. The controlled compound builder resets the exercise log state to its guarded value and clips stocks/residuals; exact continuous GBM arguments are not transferred to that changed source. Undefined numeric operations, NaNs or a nontotal program would require a different output contract rather than counting as finite real payoff values.

[Keller-Ressel, arXiv:0802.1823v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.1823v2), section 4 and section 6.1, treats moment explosion in affine stochastic-volatility models including Heston. Such a model is not a GBM lognormal marginal with fixed deterministic variance. For an uncapped Heston stock or payoff, a parameter/maturity-specific moment-domain proof is required. No such proof is carried out here; its status remains unknown, and no GBM result is assigned to it. A bounded monitored/capped payoff would still be integrable by its own support, independently of an uncapped stock's moment domain.

An arbitrary change of measure or likelihood-weighted estimator is also outside this proof: a finite unweighted payoff second moment does not establish a finite weighted second moment. No new proposal, weighting scheme, Lp estimator or Pareto toy is activated by the current contracts. If a future G0 contract supplies only a finite mean or a finite p-th moment below order two, its own tail/bias/estimator comparison is required. A divergent payoff mean would make that nominated price undefined. Nothing in this relevance audit provides a complexity, financial confidence, adoption, timing or quantum-advantage claim.
