## 0. Bottom line

1. **Where the task stands.** Take the current certified-style estimator schedule and a 100 ns logical T-layer, which is already generous. On that basis the fixed task misses the 10x inequality by **1.97e5x** in its most favourable cell (B8x52, eps = $0.10) and by up to **4.4e6x** in its least favourable cell (B4x12, $0.01). Even a hypothetical estimator constant k = 3 still leaves a miss of **554x to 1.7e4x**.
2. **The decision (is 10x reachable under any plausible combination of levers?): expected NO.** The evidence-backed levers multiply to about **67x**, which leaves a residual miss of **2.9e3x to 6.6e4x**. 10x appears only in an *optimistic corner* where three undemonstrated conditions hold at once:
   - an estimator constant k of about 3 at 99%, against 586-1,067 certified today;
   - a complete clean oracle call of about 2,700 reaction layers or fewer, against 663,704 today;
   - a logical layer time of 100 ns or less, against 446-550 ns for the best demonstrated complete loops at d = 3.

   Even that corner only reaches 10x in the cell whose classical time is a fixed-overhead artefact. In the eligible cells where classical time is not overhead-dominated, the same corner still misses by **8-41x at 100 ns**. If k is held at its best evidence-backed value (~120-220), 10x at 100 ns needs an oracle of **at most 36 reaction layers**, which is shallower than one log-depth 72-bit multiplier.
3. **What settles it formally: three numbers, about one week of screen-grade work (waves W0 + W1).**
   - (i) The idealized oracle floor (A01, with A16/A09/F05 as parameters).
   - (ii) A defensible 99% estimator-constant bracket (E01/E02/C11, with E03/E04 credits).
   - (iii) The logical-layer anchor and the Toffoli-layer recount (H01/H03).

   Decision point DP1 (§4.3) turns these into GO / GREY / NO-GO.
4. **If NO-GO (expected): finish the rigorous negative/frontier result.** It needs **2 claim-grade scopes plus 2 component-grade cards**, not more 19-step lifecycles. That is about **3-4 weeks** and ~15-20M tokens in total. 66 of the 110 items need only a one-line register record, 41 feed branch N, and 3 (C03, E07, H18) matter only on branch A.
5. **Stop now.** E11 scopes (including the #44/U4 draft), every percent-level A-family certificate, and every classical re-timing are listed in §8.

### 2.3 Verdict and what would overturn it

- **Plausible combinations (P): no.** The residual is at least 2.9e3x everywhere. No percent-level certificate (E11 L2 bridges, A-family 4-10% gains) changes this. Each moves the gap by at most 1.3x.
- **Optimistic corner (O): borderline in two overhead-artefact cells only.** These are B8x52 at $0.10 and $0.03. Once B8x52 $0.10 is charged its marginal-compute T_C (0.18 s), its O miss becomes ~2.6x. In the honest eligible cells ($0.01; B4x12 at $0.001) the miss is 8-41x at 100 ns, so 10x needs t_layer of 2.4-12 ns.
- **Overturning the expected NO needs all of the following.** (a) A 99% estimator with explicit constant k ≤ ~3-30, meaning ≤ 3 at 100 ns or ≤ ~27 at 10 ns with a 3,000-layer oracle. (b) An idealized floor of ≤ ~2,700 reaction layers per clean call at bounded width. (c) A layer clock of ≤ 100 ns. (d) A supply of ~1e12-1e13 T/s. (e) Classical held at an overhead-dominated laptop mark.

  The cheapest falsifier is (a): a literature/analytic bracket in W0-WS3, costing a fraction of a day. Next is (b), the A01 floor in W0-WS2, about 1-2 days. If either comes in above its threshold, the answer is NO for this task under any plausible lever combination.

## 5. Protocol right-sizing

### 5.1 Three rigour tiers

| Tier | Used for | Steps | Acceptance | Budget (rule of thumb) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R0 Screen** | All of W0, every kill, every register record, every optimistic bound | Spec line, then script, then JSON output with input SHA256s and measured/modelled/assumed labels, then one self-check | Reproducible rerun gives the same JSON. Any number used in a decision states its direction (quantum-favourable / adverse) | 0.2-0.5M tokens, ≤ ½ day |
| **R1 Component** | DP1 inputs (W1-R), N1-c/d, branch-A levers before adoption | R0 plus an independent recomputation by a second agent along a different code path, plus a 1-page receipt (inputs, outputs, verdict, discrepancies) | Agreement within the stated tolerance. Disagreements are resolved quantum-favourably and logged | ~1M tokens, 1 day |
| **R2 Claim-grade** | **Only** headline numbers: the F31 decision table and the A01 floor (branch N); every claimed-inequality term (branch A) | The existing ~19-step lifecycle (prospectus/preregistration, SCI, ROOT launcher, verifier, reporter, independent review, receipts), generated by the scope factory | As in scopes #40-#43 | 2-5M tokens, 2-4 days |

**Rule:** no item enters R2 unless it is a headline number of a result that is being published. Component-level results (L4) stay at R1. This removes the pattern behind scopes #40-#43, which were full lifecycles for percent-level moment bridges.

### 5.2 Batching

One R2 scope can carry **several certificates when they share inputs**. N1-a certifies C14, C04, C02, E02, F01, F02 and H16 in one lifecycle. N1-b certifies A01 and H03 together with all floor parameters. R0/R1 closures run in batches of about 20 items per agent call, from the structured rows, with one row saved per item.

### 5.3 Scope factory

The factory is built in W1-FAC under a new `research/scope_factory/` directory. Nothing frozen is touched.
- **Input:** a YAML spec per scope. Fields: scope id, rigour tier, item IDs, input artifacts with SHA256, entry script and interpreter (`frontier_t0_env` 3.12.4 `-I -S -B`), output schema, acceptance predicates (numeric tolerances, inequality direction), kill rule, reuse list of prior receipts, and a write allow-list.
- **Generated:** the mechanical lifecycle artifacts. These are the prospectus skeleton (with pre-filled hashes and predicates), the manifest, a launcher stub, a verifier that checks the outputs against the predicates, a reporter template, a receipt template and a STATUS row. The templates are copied from the #40-#43 artifacts.
- **Left to agents:** only the science script, the narrative sections of the prospectus and the independent review.
- **Expected saving:** roughly 60-70% of per-scope tokens.

### 5.4 Reuse of existing receipts

A new scope **cites** existing receipts by SHA256 instead of re-verifying them: A10_coefficient_run001, A01_run014, E07/E24/E02, C2/C5 timing, sigma_dev, G2 bias, and the 43 scope receipts. A receipt is re-verified only if its input hash changed. The register cites receipts in the same way.

### 5.5 Authorization at the stop checkpoint

- The program stays at `USER_REQUESTED_STOP_CHECKPOINT`. This plan authorizes nothing.
- Each wave opens with a **one-page wave authorization card**. The card lists the workstreams, the rigour tier per workstream, the write allow-list (e.g. `research/plan110_w0/`, `results/plan110_w0/`; never `.context/` or `results/limitation_program_20261001/` at R0/R1), the CPU runs, the agent and token caps, and the stop conditions. The user replies, for example, "authorize W0".
- R0/R1 work is authorized **per wave as a class**. Each **R2 scope is authorized individually by number.** The plan proposes repurposing #44 for N1-a (decision table) and #45 for N1-b (floor). The U4 "#44 four-target" E11 draft stays parked and unnumbered.
- DP1 and DP2 are **user decision points**. The orchestrator stops, presents the memo, and waits.

## 6. Parallelism, resources and usage limits

- **CPU-bound** (background, no agent tokens):
  - the H03 CCX-layer pass on the full sources (W0);
  - the A01 DAG reweight (light);
  - on branch A only: classical timing (lean T_C, H16 grid, C10 challengers, F06/F07) and compile experiments (A16 backend, A09 emissions).

  Timing runs never overlap compile runs. Strict idle gating, as in the Stage C runners, keeps T_C clean. Every run writes checkpointed outputs, and the orchestrator is notified when a run exits; agents do not poll.
- **Agent-bound:** ledger, floor and estimator analysis, the register, R1 recomputation, and R2 lifecycle narrative and review.
- **Usage-limit hygiene** (the last two waves of 5-9 agents hit the cap):
  - Use **3 agents by default and 4 at most**. The two heaviest workstreams (WS2 floor, WS1 ledger) start first, and the others are staggered by about 30 minutes.
  - Each agent gets a **digest** (`gap_ledger.json`, its own `rows_*.jsonl` slice, `digest_*.json`), not "read the repo". This caps context.
  - **Append-only outputs, one JSON row per sub-result.** The orchestrator's manifest skips completed rows on resume, so a cap stop costs at most one row.
  - No agent waits on a CPU run: background runs notify on exit.
  - Each wave card states a **token budget**. The wave stops at 80% and saves.
  - The register is generated from structured rows by a script, with agents reviewing in batches. Agents do not write the 66 records from scratch.
