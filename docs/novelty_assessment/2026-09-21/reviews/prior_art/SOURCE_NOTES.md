# Primary-source notes for the independent prior-art review

Date checked: 2026-09-21. Version dates below come from primary arXiv records; publication data comes from publisher pages or the publisher's Crossref deposit. Sections listed were actually inspected. These are targeted readings, not claims to have checked every theorem of every paper. No third-party full text is committed.

## Deep comparisons

### N1 — Novak: the control/residual precedent

Erich Novak, **Quantum Complexity of Integration**, *Journal of Complexity* 17(1), 2–16 (March 2001). DOI [10.1006/jcom.2000.0566](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcom.2000.0566). [arXiv:quant-ph/0008124](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0008124), v1 2000-08-29; inspected v3 2001-06-24.

Read §1.3/Theorem 1 and §§2–3, especially printed pp.4–5, 8–10; [equations (25)–(29)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0008124v3). The explicit approximation-plus-residual decomposition maps `f` to the payoff, `P_n f` to our `C(A)`, and `I(P_n f)` to our offset. The theorem assumes Hölder classes on a unit cube and function-oracle costs. Our truncated-normal weights, fixed degree-four control, signed floor arithmetic and dollar certificates are additional implementation obligations. Its rates are not a theorem about our code. No associated implementation was inspected.

### N2 — Stamatopoulos–Zeng: QSP and shifted comparison

Nikitas Stamatopoulos and William J. Zeng, **Derivative Pricing using Quantum Signal Processing**, *Quantum* 8, 1322 (2024-04-30). DOI [10.22331/q-2024-04-30-1322](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2024-04-30-1322). [arXiv:2307.14310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14310), v1 2023-07-26; inspected v2 2024-04-16.

Read [§§2–7](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14310v2), especially (9)–(17), (20)–(28), (29)–(49), Table 1 and resource assumptions. Comparator probability `(x+s)/2^p` maps to `(residual_int+H)/2^m`; multiplying back and subtracting the shift is affine inversion. Their QSP transforms a digital log-return signal; ours transforms a separable basket block. Their reported resources use Clifford+T, IQAE, an autocallable target and 68% confidence, so neither its headline ratios nor clock threshold compare directly to our CX, 95% D1/D2 plans. Q# circuit construction is described; no external code was run.

### N3 — Cibrario et al.: normalization can dominate loader savings

Francesca Cibrario, Ron Cohen, Emanuele Dri, Christian Mattia, Or Samimi Golan, Tamuz Danzig, Giacomo Ranieri, Hanan Rosemarin, Davide Corbelletto, Amir Naveh and Bartolomeo Montrucchio, **Autocallable Options Pricing with Integration-Based Exponential Amplitude Loading**. QCE 2025, pp.2459–2469; publisher deposit dated 2025-08-30. DOI [10.1109/QCE65121.2025.00267](https://doi.org/10.1109/QCE65121.2025.00267); [arXiv:2507.19039v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19039), 2025-07-25.

Read [§§II–IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19039v1), (10)–(15), (25)–(28), Table I and §IV-E; experimental scope/conclusion screened. Truncated exponential preparation improves normalization before comparator integration. Their `R` maps to our decoder sensitivity, but their autocallable payoff is not an arithmetic basket. T-depth assumes parallel preparation and a different compiler/AE model. Qmod implementation is linked as reference 25; it was not run. The IEEE DOI fetch failed; metadata was independently verified through its Crossref deposit and [official accepted-paper list](https://qce.quantum.ieee.org/2025/wp-content/uploads/sites/12/2025/07/QC25-All-Accepted-Technical-Papers.pdf). The inspected full text is the preprint, not a verified identical proceedings PDF.

### N4 — Prakash et al.: two Asian algorithms, not just nested AE

Anupam Prakash, Yue Sun, Shouvanik Chakrabarti, Charlie Che, Aditi Dandapani, Dylan Herman, Niraj Kumar, Shree Hari Sureshbabu, Ben Wood, Iordanis Kerenidis and Marco Pistoia, **Quantum option pricing via the Karhunen-Loève expansion**. [arXiv:2402.10132v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10132), 2024-02-15. No journal version was established in this search.

Read [§§2.1, 3.3, 4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10132v1), Theorems 2.4, 3.3 and 5.1; (42)–(45), (70)–(73). Its average `Gbar` maps to our `A`; `Gmax` serves payoff normalization. Section 5 has explicit exponentiation, running average, positive part, controlled amplitude rotation, uncomputation and final AE. It assumes arithmetic precision in an asymptotic analysis rather than furnishing our finite fixed-point ledger. It does not provide a matched D1/D2 CX comparison. No associated resource-estimation code was established.

### N5 — Manzano et al.: signed payoff estimation is published

Alberto Manzano, Gonzalo Ferro, Álvaro Leitao, Carlos Vázquez and Andrés Gómez, **Alternative pipeline for option pricing using quantum computers**, *EPJ Quantum Technology* 12, 28 (2025-02-25). DOI [10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00328-3](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00328-3). Earlier title **Real Option Pricing using Quantum Computers**, [arXiv:2303.06089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06089), v1 2023-03-10; v2 2024-07-17.

Read preprint §3.1, §3.2 and relevant Appendix B; checked [published §3.1 equations (26)–(30) and Appendix B (38)–(43)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00328-3). Digital payoff-to-real-amplitude encoding followed by inverse state preparation gives an expectation amplitude; mRQAE retains its sign. This differs from our shifted Bernoulli probability and canonical AE. Both handle signed expectations, defeating a broad first-signed-payoff claim. The paper cites [NEASQC/FinancialApplications](https://github.com/NEASQC/FinancialApplications); code was not executed. No same-target hardware-cost comparison follows.

### N6 — Herman et al.: explicit pricing-error framework

Dylan Herman, Yue Sun, Jin-Peng Liu, Marco Pistoia, Charlie Che, Rob Otter, Shouvanik Chakrabarti and Aram Harrow, **Quantum Speedups for Derivative Pricing Beyond Black-Scholes**. [arXiv:2602.03725v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03725), 2026-02-03; no peer-reviewed version established.

Read [§§4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03725v1#S4.SS2), (4.1)–(4.16), Theorem 4.1; inspected §5.1–Gaussian-loading statements and Appendix A.1 statement/proof passages. Their `f∘g` maps to our payoff after factor-to-price conversion; their product primitive density maps to our independent normals. They couple truncation, quadrature, arithmetic, distribution/subnormalization and AE errors. Theorem 4.1 is an asymptotic relative GBM discretization statement, not our absolute-dollar directed certificate. Their finite-density left-endpoint discretization also differs from exact normal cell-mass midpoints. No D1/D2 code comparison was performed, and the remaining Heston/CIR/MLMC proofs were not audited.

## Comparison conventions that must not be collapsed

The project's own normalization/error/AE contract is in [W2 integration](../../../../journal_sprint/MINIMAL_PIVOT_WEEK2_METHOD.md) and the [signed residual method](../../../../release/SIGNED_RESIDUAL_METHOD_20260921.md). It uses a fixed finite midpoint law with exact ideal normal cell masses, then bridges to the continuous target; explicit dollar allowances; canonical AE with 17 repetitions; controlled-oracle CX ledgers; and allocated clean scratch. These are not the same objects as asymptotic function-oracle calls, QSP payoff-component T-depth, an IQAE resource budget at another confidence level, or the elapsed time of a classical simulator.

For a matched external comparison, preserve the financial model, actual finite law, state loading, decoded slope/offset, control cost, approximation and signed arithmetic precision, cleanup and controlled operations, AE confidence, complete deterministic budget, gate compilation and ancilla allocation. Charge classical moments and phase preparation separately. Neither inspected publication nor project currently provides a common hardware mapping that makes all headline ratios comparable.
