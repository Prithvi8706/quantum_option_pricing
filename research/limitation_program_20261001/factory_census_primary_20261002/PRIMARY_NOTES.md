Litinski, *Magic State Distillation: Not as Costly as You Think*, arXiv:1905.06903v3, Table1 and Sections2–4, describes detection of all one- and two-input Pauli faults in fifteen-to-one. The often quoted35p³ is leading order. We instead use a conservative all-orders independent-input-Z bound, with perfect Clifford operations explicitly assumed. Table1 reports modeled output error4.5×10^-20 for (15-to-1)_{17,7,7}^6×(15-to-1)_{41,17,17} at physical circuit noise10^-3. Its published footprint/time metadata is73400qubits/128codecycles; these rounded modeled numbers are not this project's architecture or usable delivery rate. Storage, retries, transport, control and whole-computation failure need matching. Short excerpt: “there is no combination of two faulty gates that can go undetected.”

Gidney and Fowler, *Efficient magic state factories with a catalyzed |CCZ> to2|T> transformation*, arXiv:1812.01238v3, Sections2–3, detects individual faults in eight-to-CCZ and supplies two T outputs per consumed CCZ with a retained T catalyst. The28ε² shorthand is leading order. Direct CCZ use needs gate teleportation, classically controlled Clifford corrections, measurement and routing. Catalysis can poison the shared catalyst, correlating subsequent outputs; it is not an independent-input distillation stage. Arithmetic CCZ states and conversion CCZ states are distinct consumed opportunities. Seed, conversion and catalyst-storage faults remain separate. Short excerpt: “its errors are correlated”.
