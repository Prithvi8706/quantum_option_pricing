## Exact same-coupling setup

Let U,V be independent uniform variables on open (0,1), M = 2^32, delta = 1/M, and U_M = (floor(MU)+1/2)delta. Put

$$R(u)=\sqrt{-2\ln u},\qquad m_j=(j+1/2)\delta.$$

The radial squared error is the sum of the integrals of `(R(u)-R(m_j))^2` over the complete cells `[j*delta,(j+1)*delta]`. This changes the proof allowance, not the quantizer or source law. The endpoint u=0 is an improper-integral limit only; it is never a log-0 evaluation. At u=1, the continuous limiting value R(1)=0 suffices; no native endpoint is inserted.

For positive u,m below 1, the exact identity is

$$
(R(u)-R(m))^2
=\frac{4(\ln u-\ln m)^2}{(R(u)+R(m))^2}
=\frac{2(\ln u-\ln m)^2}{(\sqrt{-\ln u}+\sqrt{-\ln m})^2}.
$$

The original safe Holder inequality instead uses `(R(u)-R(m))^2 <= 2*abs(ln u-ln m)`. It remains available where a denominator or derivative bound is inconvenient. The following derivation keeps every cell, including both tails.

## First cell: an integrable squared-log tail

On the first cell `(0,delta]`, m = delta/2. Both R(u) and R(m) are at least R(delta). With L = ln 2, `R(delta)^2 = 64*L`, so `(R(u)+R(m))^2 >= 256*L`. Hence

$$
T_0:=\int_0^\delta (R(u)-R(\delta/2))^2du
\le\frac{1}{64L}\int_0^\delta\ln^2(2u/\delta)du
=\frac{\delta(L^2-2L+2)}{64L}.
$$

The identity follows by substituting t=u/delta and using the antiderivative `t*((ln(2t))^2-2*ln(2t)+2)`; its limit at zero is zero. Thus the first cell is included rather than truncated or sampled. The elementary integral `ln 2 = integral_1^2 dt/t` gives `0<L<1`, and therefore `L^2-2L+2<=2`. A simpler first-step allowance is

$$T_0\le\frac{\delta}{32L}.$$

This avoids adding a separate squared-log enclosure recipe. A future own-profile rational upper bound can replace L in the denominator by the existing positive lower endpoint, after its original enclosure authority is admitted.

## Interior: a complete midpoint-cell integral

For any complete cell `[a,b]` strictly inside (0,1), of width delta and midpoint m, absolute continuity and Cauchy-Schwarz give

$$
|R(u)-R(m)|^2
\le |u-m|\int_{\min(u,m)}^{\max(u,m)}|R'(s)|^2ds.
$$

Integrating over u and reversing the nonnegative integrals, the weight of a fixed s on the left half is

$$\int_a^s(m-u)du=\frac{\delta^2}{8}-\frac{(m-s)^2}{2}\le\frac{\delta^2}{8};$$

the right-half weight is the same with distance from m. Consequently

$$
\int_a^b|R(u)-R(m)|^2du
\le\frac{\delta^2}{8}\int_a^b|R'(s)|^2ds,
\qquad |R'(s)|^2=\frac{1}{2s^2(-\ln s)}.
$$

The derivative integral is not extended to the first or last cell. Its weight constant is delta^2/8, not the midpoint variance delta^2/12 or a presumed delta^2/24: the latter replacements would require a different proved inequality.

For the lower interior, use bands

$$I_k=[2^k\delta,2^{k+1}\delta],\qquad k=0,\ldots,30.$$

Every band endpoint is a multiple of delta, so each band consists of whole original cells. Together these 31 bands cover `[delta,1/2]` without crossing or omitting a cell. On I_k, `-ln s >= (31-k)*L`, and

$$
\int_{I_k}|R'(s)|^2ds
\le\frac{1}{2(31-k)L}\left(\frac{1}{2^k\delta}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}\delta}\right)
=\frac{1}{2^{k+2}\delta(31-k)L}.
$$

Summing the cell inequalities within each band gives the unevaluated candidate

$$T_{\rm lower}\le\frac{\delta}{L}\sum_{k=0}^{30}\frac{1}{2^{k+5}(31-k)}.$$

For the upper interior `[1/2,1-delta]`, its endpoints also lie on original cell boundaries. The elementary inequality `-ln s >= 1-s` and `s>=1/2` give `|R'(s)|^2 <= 2/(1-s)`. Therefore

$$
T_{\rm upper}\le\frac{\delta^2}{8}\int_{1/2}^{1-\delta}\frac{2}{1-s}ds
=\frac{31}{4}\delta^2L.
$$

The complete partition is: first cell, lower-interior whole cells, upper-interior whole cells, last cell. Shared boundary points have zero Lebesgue measure. No production-sized cell enumeration is needed for this analytic identity.

## Last cell: keep a separate Holder fallback

On `[1-delta,1]`, m = 1-delta/2. Apply the original squared square-root Holder inequality and the elementary log Lipschitz bound on this interval:

$$
(R(u)-R(m))^2
\le 2|\ln u-\ln m|
\le\frac{2|u-m|}{1-\delta}.
$$

Since `integral_(1-delta)^1 abs(u-m)du = delta^2/4`,

$$T_{\rm last}\le\frac{\delta^2}{2(1-\delta)}.$$

This is valid also at the continuous endpoint R(1)=0. It does not integrate the singular R' through 1, truncate the last cell, or introduce clipping.
