## Universal signed product and output-window proof

For sign-extended active words of widths `wa,wb`, expand each signed value as ordinary low bits with a negative coefficient on its active sign bit. In their product, a bit pair has negative weight exactly when one of its bits is an active sign bit. Both sign bits together have positive weight. This identity holds for width1 as well: its sole bit represents0 or-1. Arithmetic correctness is asserted only on the promised sign-extended span; the emitted permutation remains reversible elsewhere.

For a negative partial bit `t`, use `-t*2^k = (1-t)*2^k - 2^k`. A zero private wire receives X followed by CCX, so it holds `1 XOR (a_j AND b_i)`. A positive partial receives only CCX. The fixed correction is the sum of all negative fixed weights, reduced modulo `2^112`, and its complete set bits are prepared on fresh zero wires. Correction cannot be masked at `wa+wb` or any shorter active product width: a short negative product can require high residue ones all the way through bit111.

Terms at columns112 and above vanish modulo `2^112`, so they may be omitted. Every term in columns0..111, every fixed correction bit and all carries into the output window remain. In particular columns0..39 cannot be discarded before addition. The helper does not rely on a sum of active widths, product positivity or a sampled range to choose its modulus.

For any signed144 integer product `P`, Euclidean division gives `P = q*2^112+r` with `0 <= r < 2^112`, including negative products. Therefore `floor(P/2^40) = q*2^72 + floor(r/2^40)`. Reduction modulo `2^72` cancels the first term, so copying residue bits40..111 is exactly the original signed144 arithmetic floor40 result modulo72. This proves the negative floor and full72 output without an unsigned substitution, premature truncation or empirical point comparison.

## Retained compression, final carry and cleanup

Each CSA triple uses fresh sum and carry targets while preserving its three source wires. It first computes `p=x XOR y`; then the carry is `xy XOR (pz)`, and the sum becomes `p XOR z`. The carry predicates are disjoint, hence their XOR is the integer majority and `x+y+z = sum+2*carry`. Carry is retained in the next column whenever that column is below112. A carry above column111 may be dropped solely because the whole computation is modular112. Every old partial, correction, sum and carry wire remains available for literal reversal.

After compression reaches at most two values per column, copy them into two complete112 banks. The final explicit ripple computes propagate `left XOR right`, disjoint generate/propagate carries, and each product bit. Carry0 enters zero; all carries from column0 onward are computed. The complete112 product bank includes the unobserved low40 bits; only after this carry computation are bits40..111 copied into the dirty target.

The complete sequence is one forward compute, exactly72 output CX rows, then the literal reverse of every forward compute row. Inputs never target a gate. The output bank is untouched during compute/inverse, so inverse does not depend on its initial value. Every private wire therefore returns exactly to zero while input banks remain unchanged and the target becomes `y XOR product_window`. Every allocated wire, including unused or terminal carry storage, must be checked by the independent consumer and full-state replay.

All source rows are X, CX or ideal CCX permutations with unit amplitudes. The future lowering uses the exact15-event,7T CCX macro literal copied from K. Its independent exact cyclotomic matrix check must show all eight computational basis columns equal ideal CCX with global phase exactly1. No relative-phase or approximate CCX is allowed. Composition then gives the claimed unit-phase map on arbitrary superpositions and reference-entangled dirty outputs in the promised span. Neither a truth table alone nor forward/reverse cleanup cancels an unproved middle-output phase.
