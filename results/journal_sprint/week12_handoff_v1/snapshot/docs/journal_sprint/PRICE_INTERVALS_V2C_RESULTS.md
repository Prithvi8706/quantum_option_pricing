# Finite-shot dollar intervals: V2C results

Discovery experiment, governed by [PROTOCOL_V2C.md](PROTOCOL_V2C.md). Raw observations are generated by the declared stationary binomial response model, not noisy pricing hardware. Inputs and source snapshots are in `results/journal_sprint/price_intervals_v2c/`; independent reconstruction and denominator-aware summaries are in `results/journal_sprint/week12_closeout_v1/`.

## Verified execution

There are 43,200 unique attempt records in 216 cells of 200 trials: 36,000 executed response simulations and 7,200 predetermined refusals. States are 11,814 precision-met, 24,032 unresolved, 154 incompatible, and 7,200 pre-refused. Across all conditions/budgets, 12 erroneous declarations occurred; this heterogeneous aggregate is descriptive, not a single IID reliability experiment.

Every stored observation vector was checked for valid counts and its amplitude set recomputed. Every nonempty price interval, declared radius, containment indicator and erroneous-declaration indicator was reconstructed. All A-equivalent, Grover and shot totals matched their schedules. The archived V2A successful retry, V2B and V2C manifests verified 23, 24 and 27 artifacts respectively. The earlier failed V2A attempt remains retained separately.

`pooled.csv` contains the same summary for every budget; `cells.json` retains contract-level counts and marginal exact binomial intervals. No pooled IID confidence interval is assigned to the six heterogeneous contracts. Zero observed false declarations does not prove zero risk: in a 200-trial cell, zero events has a two-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper endpoint of about 1.83%.

At 512 shots, fixed ideal multidepth has 3 false declarations among 1,200 attempts and 207 declarations: 0.25% unconditionally and 1.45% among declarations. This distinction is retained throughout the analysis. Statistical failure events can also create empty/incompatible sets even when the response model is correct; incompatibility alone does not prove model mismatch.

## What selection did

The fixed arm uses n=6,c=.125 for every contract. The bound-only selector chooses n=6,c=.25 for E001, n=6,c=.125 for E014/E025/E049, and refuses E030/E038 before sampling. It receives only candidate representation fields, sensitivity and deterministic bounds, never exact prices or target amplitudes.

At the largest budget, the selected arm's mean A-equivalent cost per attempt is 764,586.67, versus 1,146,880 for fixed. This one-third reduction is explained by one-third of attempts refusing before execution. Among executed cases, cost is unchanged. This is not evidence of a superior adaptive schedule or a measured practical quantum speedup.

Refusals must not be counted as failures in an interval-coverage denominator while calling that number coverage. The original raw summary field `price_containment_rate` divides by all attempts. It remains unchanged for provenance; the closeout analysis supplies explicitly named executed/nonempty containment rates alongside refusal-inclusive delivery.

## Runtime and scope

V2C response simulation took 45.34 seconds on the local host. Cached-bound selection took approximately .00015 seconds, but this excludes constructing the bounds. A separate five-repeat reconstruction of all 72 candidate bounds took .825–1.081 seconds per matrix. Those timings are post-run CPU diagnostics, not full pricing-circuit setup, calibration or quantum execution costs. Raw files contain more precise values.

The final interpretation is positive but limited: deterministic pricing error can be propagated into useful finite-shot dollar intervals for some pilot contracts. A fixed multidepth procedure delivers more often than equal-A-query direct quantum sampling at the largest tested budget. The experiment does not establish superiority over modern AE, strong classical pricing algorithms, or hardware. All six contracts were used for discovery.
