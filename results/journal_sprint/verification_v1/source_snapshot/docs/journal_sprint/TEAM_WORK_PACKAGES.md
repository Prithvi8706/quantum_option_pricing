# Small, substantive collaborator packages

Author's preference: the lead does the main research and implementation; the two
collaborators receive limited, clearly bounded work. No names have been added.
The hour estimates below are planning estimates, not authorship thresholds.

## Lead researcher

Own the research question, method, implementation, main experiments, classical
baselines, interpretation and first manuscript draft. Maintain the evidence
archive and decide whether the eventual findings justify the proposed claims.

## Collaborator 1: independent comparator reproduction

Estimated initial commitment: 3–5 hours, plus a later manuscript review.

1. Read the comparator's methods and the sprint's convention/accounting notes.
2. Run the pinned source benchmark independently in a new output directory.
3. Check the golden results, amplitude-versus-probability distinction and resource
   counts. Inspect one raw-output case rather than just copying the summary.
4. Write a short interpretation: what was reproduced, what was not, discrepancies,
   and one concrete correction or justified confirmation of the comparison.
5. Draft or critically revise the comparator paragraph and review the final claims
   that depend on that work.

Deliverables: their run manifest/results, a signed/date-stamped technical note and
their manuscript revisions. They should perform this work themselves; the lead
or assistant preparing everything under their name is not independent review.

## Collaborator 2: statistical validity and limitations

Estimated initial commitment: 3–5 hours, plus a later manuscript review. Requires
comfort with basic probability; if that is not their background, reassign rather
than asking them to approve material they cannot evaluate.

1. Read the fixed-schedule containment argument and the observed failure cases.
2. Check the difference between coverage, precision declarations, conditional
   false-declaration rates and the treatment of incompatible trials.
3. Independently inspect one matched-noise and one misspecified-noise result from
   raw records; explain why a nonempty confidence set does not validate the model.
4. Produce a short substantive critique and propose any needed wording or analysis
   correction. Help write the assumptions/limitations subsection.
5. Review the final interpretation and confirm that their issues were resolved.

Deliverables: a technical review note, a traceable checked example and manuscript
revisions. This is intellectual review/interpretation, not formatting or spelling.

## Authorship checkpoint

These are proposed contributions, not guaranteed author slots. At submission,
check the selected journal's actual authorship criteria against completed work.
Each author must approve the final manuscript and accept the required
accountability. Record contributions accurately; do not invent conceptualization,
software, experiments or supervision roles. If someone's actual help does not
meet the criteria, acknowledge it instead of listing them as an author.

Names, acceptance of the packages and actual availability remain to be supplied.
No external assignment or message has been sent on the author's behalf.
