# Longer abstract for the generic submission form

Quantum mean-estimation guarantees do not by themselves establish a practical
option-pricing advantage. We examine a controlled compound Asian-basket pricing
implementation with an explicit finite probability law, reversible arithmetic
source, strong classical comparisons and finite-confidence estimation schedules.
Sound integer ranges for four development models justify narrower exact
multipliers. Parallel schedules pay for private workspace and shared-input
copies, reducing source T depth by factors of approximately 38 and 108 on two
compiled cases. A concrete Hadamard-test implementation reduces the controlled
iteration counts of the preceding quantum implementation by factors exceeding
one thousand; a bounded-output amplitude estimator avoids its conditional
second-moment assumption. We combine emitted gate counts with actual rotation
synthesis and distinguish constructed schedules from necessary capacity bounds.
Despite these improvements, the studied implementation and stated encoded
resource models do not establish the declared tenfold classical crossover.
Finite-law and joint arithmetic bounds do not complete the implemented-policy
regret and baseline-confidence obligations. The contribution is an auditable
digital-oracle feasibility case study, not a new mean-estimation algorithm,
hardware demonstration or proof that quantum advantage is impossible.
